# Case Study 01 — Aftermath of El Mencho Operation (Jalisco, Mexico)
**Title**: Aftermath of the 22 February 2026 military operation against CJNG leader El Mencho in Jalisco, Mexico.  
**Date**: November 7 2026  
**Analyst**: Daniel Andrés  
**Confidence**: High  

## 1. Context & Research Question
On 22 February 2026, Mexican military forces conducted an operation in Guadalajara, Jalisco, that resulted in the death of Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes (“El Mencho”), leader of the Cártel Jalisco Nueva Generación (CJNG). In response, CJNG-linked actors carried out widespread retaliatory actions across Jalisco and other states, including vehicle arson used as roadblocks.  

**Research question**: Can open-source reporting and visual analysis confirm that the attached image depicts the aftermath of these events in or near the Guadalajara metropolitan area?

## 2. Sources & Collection Method
- Google Images reverse search of the attached photo (conducted 7 November 2026) — returned multiple news articles linking the scene to the 22 February 2026 events in Jalisco.
- El Informador (25 February 2026): Governor of Jalisco stated 266 vehicles were burned across the state.  
- Debate.com.mx (23 February 2026): Early reporting of vehicle burnings and detentions in the Guadalajara metropolitan area.  
- Multiple international outlets (AP, Reuters, The Guardian, etc.) confirming widespread vehicle arson in Jalisco on 22–23 February 2026.  
- Google Maps / Street View comparison of Guadalajara-area streets.  

**Language**: Spanish (primary local reporting) and English (international wires).  
**Tools**: Google Images, Google Search, Google Maps.

## 3. Findings
- The image shows a Mexican police officer standing in front of two burned vehicles on a wide urban road.
- The vehicles appear deliberately set on fire (consistent with reported roadblock tactics).
- The scene matches the style and timing of dozens of other photos published from Guadalajara and surrounding areas on 22–23 February 2026.
- Reverse image search and contemporaneous reporting from aforementioned news sources place the broader event firmly in Jalisco, with heavy concentration in the Guadalajara metropolitan area.

## 4. Assessment
The image is consistent with the documented wave of retaliatory vehicle arson carried out by CJNG-linked actors immediately after the death of El Mencho. High confidence that the photo was taken in Guadalajara, Jalisco, immediately on 22 February 2026.  

Visible TTPs include the use of stolen or seized civilian vehicles as improvised roadblocks through arson — a pattern previously observed in CJNG responses to high-profile operations like the intelligence raid on a clandestine cartel meeting in Ixtlahuacán del Río, back in August 2022. This tactic is known as narcobloqueos (narco-blockades)

## 5. Gaps & Limitations
- Exact street or neighborhood has not been confirmed.
- It is not possible to determine from the image alone whether the vehicles were private cars or commercial/public transport.
- No independent geolocation verification (e.g., matching unique landmarks) has been completed yet.

## 6. Implications for CTI / Private Sector
Companies with supply chains, logistics routes, or physical assets in Jalisco (and more broadly in western Mexico) should monitor CJNG leadership transitions closely. Retaliatory vehicle arson and roadblocks can disrupt ground transportation for days and create secondary security risks for personnel and cargo.
